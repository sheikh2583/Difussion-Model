\begin{abstract}
This project studies how the target learned by a continuous-time generative model
and the distribution used to sample training time affect image quality and
inference cost. Three unconditional CIFAR-10 generative systems are implemented
within one algorithm-agnostic pipeline: Flow Matching (FM) with uniform time
sampling, FM with logit-normal time sampling (FM-LN), and Mean Flow (MF) with
interval-conditioned average velocity. All three share a single
6{,}352{,}899-parameter time-conditioned SimpleUNet backbone; MF adds 323
parameters for the second interval endpoint.

The system records training loss, wall-clock timing, GPU memory, epoch
checkpoints, sample grids, Fr\'echet Inception Distance (FID), and Inception
Score (IS), and exposes trained checkpoints through interactive training-history
and live-inference browser views.

The strongest submitted configuration is logit-normal FM at 20 function
evaluations (NFE), achieving FID~72.66 and IS~$6.18\!\pm\!0.52$ on 1{,}000
samples. Standard FM reaches FID~76.10 and IS~$6.12\!\pm\!0.72$. MF achieves
the best single-step result (FID~210.99, IS~$2.54\!\pm\!0.21$) but its loss
rises over 30 epochs and additional NFE does not improve quality. Under the
submitted training budgets, logit-normal FM is the strongest multi-step system,
while MF requires further optimisation before its intended one-step benefit
becomes competitive. Because batch size, epochs, learning rate, hardware context,
and sample count are not fully matched across runs, all results are reported as
audited engineering evidence rather than a universal algorithm ranking.
\end{abstract}
